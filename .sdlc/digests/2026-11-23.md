## Step 4: a player's week by category

- **S0, the yardstick.** Today's projection beats the naive manager (season average × scheduled games) in every category in both leagues; NBA points, for example, 29.1 against 30.9 error a player-week. Every bar is a quarter of its noise, with no category worse.
- **S1, games actually played: NBA open, WNBA failed.** Counting back-to-back second nights at a player's own habit of sitting them cut the NBA's points error by 0.84 a week against a bar of 0.15. But a flat discount on every game gets about 70% of that, because the historical boards lack the injury listing (#1088), and the test's schedule is the one as played (#1087). It waits on 2026-27's live weeks. In the WNBA back-to-backs are too rare to matter.
- **S2, minutes when a teammate is out: failed in both leagues.** Giving an absent starter's minutes to his teammates made their week worse at every setting, because their weeks are already overstated by the games they miss themselves. Not tested: an absence announced before he has missed a game, since the history lacks the listing.
- **S3, opportunity before outcome: failed (NBA only).** Rebounds, assists and steals from tracking chances were worse than the projection, and blocks no better. They beat a player's last few games, but so does his plain three-season average.
- **S4, shot diet: failed in both leagues.** FG% from recent shot zones gained a little (past its bar on its own in the WNBA, not in the NBA); threes got worse, and the study needed both.

Fails are recorded and not retried. Each study has a page in plain words in docs/analysis.
