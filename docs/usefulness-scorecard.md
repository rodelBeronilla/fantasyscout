# In-season usefulness scorecard

Date: 2026-11-08. Scope: the eight in-season jobs a manager does; drafting and preparation are out of scope for now. Each job was walked end to end on FantasyScout (its code and its live pages) and compared with Yahoo Fantasy, ESPN Fantasy, Basketball Monster, Hashtag Basketball and League Winner Sports.

**How the comparison was made.** Competitors' free pages were walked live. Their paid features were judged from their own published help and feature pages, without logging in, and are marked unverified where only marketing copy describes them. None of the competitors document how their pages differ on a phone and a desktop, so digestibility is judged in depth for FantasyScout only.

**Where FantasyScout leads:**
- trade judgement on both sides and in categories won, with a league-wide finder;
- expected games missed calibrated on past injury listings;
- your team's categories as win chances;
- WNBA coverage;
- free throughout.

**Where it trails:**
- **The daily jobs:** today's lineup, and the week's matchup while it is under way.
- **Freshness:** keeping the league copy current. ESPN is a one-time snapshot, the Yahoo import waits on Yahoo's approval, and the week's opponent is picked by hand.

## Qualitative data on other platforms

- **Yahoo:** licensed player notes and push alerts.
- **ESPN:** short unsourced blurbs beside rostered-share changes, and weekly columns.
- **Basketball Monster:** a raw status feed linking to the posts behind it.
- **Hashtag Basketball:** next to no news.
- **League Winner:** a newsroom framed by fantasy impact, and a one-line note per waiver candidate.

FantasyScout carries:
- ESPN headlines, news flags and injury notes;
- the official injury reports and lineup tags;
- add and drop trends;
- weekly AI notes whose every number is traced.

It has no per-player written notes yet. The qualitative-signals epic (#812) covers that.
